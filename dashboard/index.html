Token Launch Radar · model radar-3

Which pre-token protocol is worth farming next?

Every protocol in the sealed DefiLlama snapshot that still has no token and could plausibly launch one, ranked by what its launch would be worth on the market's own size curve — each with a value range, an airdrop yardstick, and the concrete steps to farm it before it issues one.

How the radar values a launch

The market does not pay one price per dollar of TVL. A dollar locked in a $5M protocol prices very differently from a dollar locked in a $5B one, so radar-3 fits the curve the snapshot's own trading protocols trace: an ordinary-least-squares line of log10(market cap ÷ TVL) against log10(TVL) over every tokenized protocol with a market cap and at least $1M of TVL, exchanges excluded. A pre-token protocol is first priced by that curve at its own size: curve multiple = 10 ^ (level + slope × log10(TVL)).

A category may then bend the curve — only as far as its own trading peers earn. Each category's effect is the median of its peers' residuals around the curve, shrunk toward ×1 with four pseudo-peers (effect = (n × median residual) / (n + 4) in log space) and clamped to ×0.4–×2.5, so a category with a single loud peer can lean on the curve but never break it. Each protocol is then scored by one function of its own record: implied value = TVL × curve multiple × category effect × growth × maturity, where growth = 1 + 0.75 × clamp(7-day TVL change, −40%, +60%) and maturity = 0.55 + 0.45 × min(days running with no token, 730) / 730 (an unrecorded age counts as 365 days). A protocol that is growing and has operated longer without a token ranks above an identical one that is shrinking or brand new.

The model grades itself in the open. A leave-one-out backtest reprices every trading protocol as if it were tokenless — refitting the curve and its category effect without it — and reports the median multiplicative miss next to the same number for a flat snapshot-wide median multiple. The fitted curve, both misses, and the middle half of the model's own errors are in the calibration panel below; the middle half is also the range printed on every row. Treat the range, not the point, as the estimate.

Every row shows its own factors and a confidence grade: high when its category has at least eight trading peers and the record is complete, medium with at least three peers and at most one missing figure, low otherwise. Discount low-confidence rows; the snapshot speaks thinly about them. The airdrop yardstick on each row answers a narrower question: if a launch ever gave 10% of supply to depositors pro rata, what would $1,000 farmed map to at the modelled value? It is an assumption for sizing positions, stated so it can be disagreed with — not a forecast of any distribution.

What the board excludes, and why

Protocols whose token already trades are never ranked by the model — they sit in the “Live tokens” view at the market's own values. Of the tokenless remainder, the board sets aside every record that is a claim on somebody else's token story rather than an independent launch: centralised exchanges; bridges of every kind, including a chain's own canonical bridge (the token in its story is the chain's token, and several of those records in this snapshot even carry the chain token's market cap); liquid staking and restaking receipts and staking pools, which are claims on assets that already trade; stablecoin wrappers; versioned deployments whose name ends in a release marker (V2, v3, 1.5), which are pools of another protocol; wrapped products of a trading asset (a name carrying “Wrapped”, or a wrapped-asset ticker in the name); products of a listed exchange group; and a tokenless record that shares its name with a record that already carries a token. Every set-aside record is listed, with its reason, in the “Set aside” view — nothing is dropped silently. What remains is ranked whole: the watchlist of protocols above $50M TVL is the floor of the board, never its ceiling, so the handful of watchlist members that sit in set-aside categories are ranked anyway and flagged in their detail view.

Honesty box

An implied value is this model's claim about relative worth, not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when, or what it will trade at — the backtest above says plainly how far off the model runs even on protocols whose tokens exist. Farm plans are generic playbooks derived from each protocol's category, chains, size and age — check a protocol's own site for any live points programme before acting, and never pay anyone to “claim” an airdrop. Figures come from one sealed snapshot; they do not move after its date.
